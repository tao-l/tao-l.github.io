Course overview

How do we design and analyze systems whose participants act in their own interests? Algorithmic game theory lies at the intersection of theoretical computer science, mathematics, and economics. This course studies strategic behavior, the efficiency of decentralized systems, and mechanisms that are robust to strategic manipulation, with an emphasis on algorithms and computational complexity.

Topics include the price of anarchy, auctions and contracts, stable matching and fair allocation, learning in games, equilibrium computation and complexity, information elicitation and design, and cooperative game theory.

Prerequisites: An advanced undergraduate or graduate course in algorithms or theory of computation, discrete mathematics, and probability.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  1. Recognize strategic behavior and incentive issues in real-world applications.
  2. Propose a game-theoretic model for a real-world phenomenon.
  3. Apply concepts from algorithmic game theory to analyze equilibria, learning dynamics, and the inefficiency arising from players’ strategic behavior in the proposed model.
  4. Design a mechanism (such as an auction, a contract, or an information structure) to achieve certain objectives.
  5. Analyze and evaluate the proposed mechanism using appropriate criteria, such as welfare, revenue, incentive compatibility, and computational complexity.
